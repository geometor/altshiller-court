\(a) Let :g:`AD <0079.g1:/ A D />` be the altitude and :g:`AK <0079.g1:/ A K />` the circumdiameter issued from the vertex :g:`A <0079.g1:A>` (Fig. 44). The angles :g:`B <0079.g1:< A B D )>` and :g:`K <0079.g1:< A K C )>` are equal since they subtend

.. picture:: 0079.g1.svg
   :alt: Fig. 44 — Circumdiameter AK through circumcenter O, altitude AD, bisector AU, and median AA' of triangle ABC
   :caption: FIG. 44

the same :g:`chord <0079.g1:/ A C />` of the :term:`circumcircle <circumcircle>`; hence we have from the right triangles :g:`ABD <0079.g1:< A B D >>`, :g:`ACK <0079.g1:< A C K >>`:

.. math::

   \angle BAD = \angle KAC = 90^\circ - B,

and:

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \angle DAK = A - 2(90^\circ - B) = A + 2B - 180^\circ &= \\
   A + 2B - A - B - C &= B - C.
   \end{aligned}

The student may verify that the proposition holds when one of the angles :math:`B, C` is obtuse.

\(b) Let :g:`AU <0079.g1:/ A U />` be the bisector of the angle :math:`A`. Since :eq:`∠BAD = CAO <0079.g1:< B A D ) = 0079.g1:< K A C )>`, we have angle :eq:`DAU = OAU <0079.g1:< D A U ) = 0079.g1:< O A U )>`.

.. enunciation::

   **74. Corollary.** *In the right triangle* :g:`ADU <0079.g1:< A D U >>` *we have* :math:`AD = h_a`\ *,* :math:`AU = t_a`\ *, and angle* :math:`DAU = \tfrac{1}{2}(B - C)`\ *, i.e., we have the datum:*

   .. math::

      h_a,\quad t_a,\quad B - C.

.. enunciation::

   **75. Theorem.** *The difference of the two angles which an* :g:`*internal bisector* <0079.g1:/ A U />` *of an angle of a* :g:`*triangle* <0079.g1:< A B C >>` *makes with the* :g:`*opposite side* <0079.g1:/ B C />` *is equal to the difference of the angles adjacent to this side.*

From the two triangles :g:`AUB <0079.g1:< A U B >>`, :g:`AUC <0079.g1:< A U C >>` (Fig. 44) we have:

.. math::

   \angle AUC = B + \tfrac{1}{2}A, \quad \angle AUB = C + \tfrac{1}{2}A;

hence:

.. math::

   \angle AUC - \angle AUB = B - C.
