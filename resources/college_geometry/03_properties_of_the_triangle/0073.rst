**56. Remark II.** If :math:`p > q`, we have :math:`AC > CB` and :math:`AD > DB`, so that the midpoint :g:`M <0072.g1:M>` of :g:`AB <0072.g1:/ A B />` lies outside the segment :g:`CD <0072.g1:/ C D />`. The same relative position of the points :g:`M <0072.g1:M>`, :g:`C <0072.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0072.g1:D>` prevails, if :math:`p < q`.

If :math:`p = q`, the point :g:`C <0072.g1:C>` coincides with :g:`M <0072.g1:M>`, and there is no external point of division, since :g:`FH <0072.g1:[ F H ]>` is then parallel to :g:`AB <0072.g1:[ A B ]>`.

.. enunciation::

   **57. Theorem.** *If the points* :g:`C <0072.g1:C>`\ *,* :g:`D <0072.g1:D>` *divide the segment* :g:`AB <0072.g1:/ A B />` *internally and externally in the ratio* :math:`p : q`\ *, the points* :g:`B <0072.g1:B>`\ *,* :g:`A <0072.g1:A>` *divide the segment* :g:`DC <0072.g1:/ C D />` *internally and externally in the ratio* :math:`(p + q) : (p - q)`\ *.*

We have (Fig. 39):

.. math::

   AC : CB = p : q, \quad AD : DB = p : q;

hence:

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   (1) \quad & (AC + CB) : CB = (p + q) : q, & (AD - DB) : DB &= (p - q) : q, \\
   (2) \quad & AC : (AC + CB) = p : (p + q), & AD : (AD - DB) &= p : (p - q).
   \end{aligned}

.. assertion::

   Substituting :g:`AB <0072.g1:/ A B />` for :math:`AC + CB` and for :math:`AD - DB` in (1) and (2), and combining the two proportions in each of these two lines we obtain:

   .. math::

      \begin{aligned}
      DB : BC &= (p + q) : (p - q), \\
      DA : AC &= (p + q) : (p - q).
      \end{aligned}

.. enunciation::

   **58. Corollary.** *If* :math:`AB = a` *and* :math:`CD = b`\ *, we have* :math:`b = 2apq : (p^2 - q^2)`\ *.*

Indeed, we have:

.. math::

   AD : AB = p : (p - q), \quad AC : AB = p : (p + q), \quad CD = AD - AC;

hence the announced result.

**59. Definitions.** Instead of saying that the points :g:`C <0072.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0072.g1:D>` (§ 57) divide the segment :g:`AB <0072.g1:/ A B />` internally and externally in the same ratio, we shall sometimes say that the points :g:`C <0072.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0072.g1:D>` *divide the segment* :math:`AB` :term:`*harmonically* <harmonic-division>`, or that the points :g:`C <0072.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0072.g1:D>` *separate the points* :math:`A, B` *harmonically*, or that the points :g:`C <0072.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0072.g1:D>` are :term:`*harmonic conjugates* <harmonic-division>` with respect to the points :g:`A <0072.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0072.g1:B>`.

The preceding proposition (§ 57) states that the relation between the two pairs of points is mutual, so that the points :g:`A <0072.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0072.g1:B>`, in turn, divide the segment :g:`CD <0072.g1:/ C D />` harmonically, and the points :g:`A <0072.g1:A>`, :g:`B <0072.g1:B>` separate the points :g:`C <0072.g1:C>`, :g:`D <0072.g1:D>` harmonically. We may thus refer to the two pairs of points :math:`A, B` and :math:`C, D` as *two pairs of harmonic points*, and to the two segments :g:`AB <0072.g1:/ A B />`, :g:`CD <0072.g1:/ C D />` as *two harmonic segments*.
