Harmonic Ranges, Pencils, and Complete Quadrangles¶

Chapter VII: Harmonic Division • Articles §§ 345–357 (Folios 0185–0190, pp. 166–171)

Elevates harmonic division (§§ 345–357, Folios 0185–0190, pp. 166–171) from a metric segment ratio to the fundamental projective invariant of harmonic pencils, complete quadrangles, and complete quadrilaterals (Pappus VII, Chasles).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 345–349 — Algebraic & Midpoint Characterizations: Proves the equivalence of \((A,B;C,D) = -1\) with the harmonic mean \(\frac{2}{AB} = \frac{1}{AC} + \frac{1}{AD}\) and Newton's midpoint relation \(MA^2 = MB^2 = MC \cdot MD\).

  • §§ 350–353 — Harmonic Pencils & Gauss–Newton Line: Any transversal cuts a harmonic pencil \(O(ABCD)\) in four harmonic points; a transversal parallel to one ray is bisected by the other three; midpoints of the three diagonals of a complete quadrilateral are collinear (Gauss–Newton line).

  • §§ 354–357 — Complete Quadrangle & Straightedge Harmonic Construction: Opposite sides of a complete quadrangle meet a diagonal line in harmonic conjugates, constructing the fourth harmonic point with the straightedge alone!

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Directly unites geometor/pappus (projective cross-ratio invariance) and geometor/divine (automated detection of harmonic and golden ranges), illustrated in the harmonic_quadrangle hero figure.
