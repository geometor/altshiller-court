C. Geometric Loci¶

The method of intersecting geometric loci (§ 11), twelve fundamental linear and circular loci including the capable arc and Apollonian circle (Loci 1–12, Figs. 8–12), worked locus constructions (§§ 12–14, Figs. 13–16), and 27 locus and construction exercises.

§ 11. Important Loci

In a great many cases the solution of a geometric problem depends upon the finding of a point which satisfies certain conditions. For instance, in order to draw a circle passing through three given points, it is necessary to find a point, the center of the circle, equidistant from the three given points.

§ 11. Exercises (1–4)

A variable parallel to the base \(BC\) of a triangle \(ABC\) meets \(AB, AC\) in \(D, E\). Show that the locus of the point \(M = (BE, CD)\) is a straight line.

§ 12. Problem

Draw a circle passing through two given points and subtending a given angle at a third given point.

§ 13. Problem

Through two given points of a circle to draw two parallel chords whose sum shall have a given length.

§ 14. Problem

On a given circle to find a point such that the lines joining it to two given points on this circle shall meet a given line in two points the ratio of whose distances from a given point on this line shall have a given value.
