Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

In § 17’s Discussion, because \(BC = a < b + c = BD\) in auxiliary \(\triangle BCD\), the known angle \(\angle D = \frac{1}{2}A\) lies opposite the shorter side \(a\), producing the classic SSA (ambiguous case) bifurcation (two, one, or zero triangles depending on \(a \gtrless (b + c)\sin\frac{1}{2}A\)). Moreover, the identity \(\angle BCD = 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2}(B - C)\) couples the side-sum \(b + c\) directly to the angle-difference \(B - C\), powering Exercises 6–9 and anticipating Mollweide’s formula.
