Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

In § 15 (completed at the top of this folio), auxiliary triangle \(AEF\) is constructed from \((2p, 90^\circ + \frac{1}{2}A, h_a)\) by intersecting the capable arc on \(EF\) with the parallel at height \(h_a\), yielding two symmetric, one tangent, or zero intersections; vertices \(B, C\) are then recovered via the mediators of \(AE, AF\). Section 17 applies the same unfolding principle to a single pair of sides: laying off \(AD = AC\) along \(BA\) produced creates isosceles \(\triangle ACD\), halving the exterior vertical angle so \(\angle ADC = \frac{1}{2}A\) opposite the known base \(BC = a\) in auxiliary \(\triangle BCD\) with side \(BD = b + c\).

Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

In § 17’s Discussion, because \(BC = a < b + c = BD\) in auxiliary \(\triangle BCD\), the known angle \(\angle D = \frac{1}{2}A\) lies opposite the shorter side \(a\), producing the classic SSA (ambiguous case) bifurcation (two, one, or zero triangles depending on \(a \gtrless (b + c)\sin\frac{1}{2}A\)). Moreover, the identity \(\angle BCD = 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2}(B - C)\) couples the side-sum \(b + c\) directly to the angle-difference \(B - C\), powering Exercises 6–9 and anticipating Mollweide’s formula.
