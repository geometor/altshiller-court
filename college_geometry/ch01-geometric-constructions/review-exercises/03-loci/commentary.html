Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

  • Item 55: Classical Euclidean quadratic identity (Elements II.9 and II.10), \(AD^2 + DB^2 = 2(AC^2 + CD^2)\), the one-dimensional specialization of Apollonius’s median theorem (§ 11, Locus 10).

  • Item 57: The familiar five-square dissection proving \(\text{area}(\text{inner square}) = \frac{1}{5}\,\text{area}(ABCD)\).

  • Item 58: Blanchet’s theorem—for any cevian pair \(BM, CN\) concurring on altitude \(AD\), the altitude \(AD\) bisects \(\angle MDN\) (a harmonic-bundle projection property revisited in Chapter VII).

Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

Chapter I closes with five locus and fixed-point concurrency problems that bridge directly into Chapter II (Similitude and Homothecy) and Chapter VIII (Circles):

  • Item 60 (on folio 0051) yields the circle of similitude (Apollonian circle of the centers of similitude).

  • Item 64 exhibits stereographic-tangent / pole-and-polar linearity (\(BM\) projects \((O)\) onto diameter \(AA'\), and \(NP = NM\) yields a fixed 45° line through \(B'\)).

  • Item 67 applies independent axial scalings along \(OA\) and \(OB\) (\(OA'/OA = \lambda\), \(OB'/OB = \mu\)), mapping the pencil of lines through \(P(x_0, y_0)\) in oblique coordinates \((OA, OB)\) to the pencil through \(P'(\lambda x_0, \mu y_0)\).
