Chapter II: Similitude and Homothecy¶

Method of Similitude, Homothetic Figures, Direct and Inverse Centers of Similitude, Circle of Similitude, Pantograph

Transformational construction and proof via similitude and homothecy (§§ 23–53, Folios 0053–0071, pp. 34–52): homothetic figures, direct and inverse centers of similitude of two circles, the circle of similitude, and the pantograph.

Section A — Similitude

Introduces the constructive method of similitude (§§ 23–30): when all conditions save one linear dimension determine the shape (species) of a figure, a convenient similar prototype is constructed first and then scaled to satisfy the remaining boundary or metric condition (Euclid VI ).

Section B — Homothecy

Develops central dilation or homothecy mathcal{H}(S, k) (§§ 31–53) as a point-to-point geometric transformation preserving parallelism, angles, and ratio of lengths, culminating in the centers of similitude and circle of similitude of two circles.

See Commentary on Chapter II: Similitude and Homothecy.
