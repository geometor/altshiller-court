Section B — Homothecy¶

Chapter II: Similitude and Homothecy • Articles §§ 31–53 (Folios 0057–0071, pp. 38–52)

Develops central dilation or homothecy \(\mathcal{H}(S, k)\) (§§ 31–53) as a point-to-point geometric transformation preserving parallelism, angles, and ratio of lengths, culminating in the centers of similitude and circle of similitude of two circles.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 31–38 — Homothetic Figures & Homothetic Center: Homologous segments are parallel and proportional (\(S P' / S P = k\)); any two unequal parallel segments or circles are both directly (\(k > 0\)) and inversely (\(k < 0\)) homothetic.

  • §§ 39–45 — Direct & Inverse Centers of Similitude of Two Circles: Two unequal circles \((O, R)\) and \((O', R')\) possess external and internal centers of similitude \(S, S'\) dividing \(OO'\) harmonically in ratio \(R : R'\), through which common tangents and antihomologous secants pass.

  • §§ 46–53 — Circle of Similitude & The Pantograph: Proves that the locus of points from which two unequal circles subtend equal angles is the Apollonian circle on diameter \(SS'\), and analyzes Scheiner’s mechanical pantograph linkage.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Applied to a triangle’s circumcircle and incircle in geometor/etc, the internal and external similicenters yield Kimberling centers \(X(55)\) and \(X(56)\).
