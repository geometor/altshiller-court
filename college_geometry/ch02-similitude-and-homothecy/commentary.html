Commentary on Chapter II: Similitude and Homothecy¶

Back to Chapter II: Similitude and Homothecy.

Constructive Analysis, Historical Provenance & GEOMETOR Notes (0053–0071, pp. 34–52)

Mathematical Architecture & Transformation Theory¶

Chapter II introduces the first great transformational pillar of College Geometry: similitude (§§ 23–30) and homothecy (§§ 31–53). Where Chapter I constructs a figure directly at its final position and scale, the method of similitude temporarily sets aside one linear metric condition, constructs a convenient prototype of the required shape (species), and scales it into place from a well-chosen center of homothecy.

  • Homothetic Centers of Two Circles (§§ 39–45): Any two unequal circles \((O, R)\) and \((O', R')\) admit two centers of similitude—external (direct, ratio \(+R/R'\)) and internal (inverse, ratio \(-R/R'\))—which divide the line of centers \(OO'\) harmonically.

  • Circle of Similitude & Pantograph (§§ 46–53): The locus of points from which two given unequal circles subtend equal angles is the Apollonian circle having their two centers of similitude as diameter (the circle of similitude), coaxal with the two given circles.

GEOMETOR Ecosystem Connections¶

  • Triangle Centers (``geometor/etc``): Applied to the circumcircle and incircle of \(\triangle ABC\), the internal and external centers of similitude are Kimberling centers \(X(55)\) (insimilicenter) and \(X(56)\) (exsimilicenter), studied by Euler, Monge, and Poncelet.

  • Medial & Anticomplementary Homothecy: At the centroid \(G = X(2)\), the homothecy \(\mathcal{H}(G, -\tfrac{1}{2})\) maps \(\triangle ABC\) onto its complementary (medial) triangle, driving the synthetic proofs of the Euler line and nine-point circle in Chapter III.
