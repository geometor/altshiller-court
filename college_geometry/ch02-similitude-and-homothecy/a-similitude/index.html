Section A — Similitude¶

Chapter II: Similitude and Homothecy • Articles §§ 23–30 (Folios 0053–0057, pp. 34–38)

Introduces the constructive method of similitude (§§ 23–30): when all conditions save one linear dimension determine the shape (species) of a figure, a convenient similar prototype is constructed first and then scaled to satisfy the remaining boundary or metric condition (Euclid VI).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 23–25 — Principle of Auxiliary Similar Prototypes: Suppressing one linear datum converts a rigid placement problem into a one-parameter family of similar figures sharing fixed angular and ratio invariants.

  • §§ 26–28 — Inscribed Squares & Given-Shape Triangles: Classic constructions inscribing a square in a given triangle \(ABC\) and inscribing a triangle of given angles or side ratios inside another triangle or circle.

  • §§ 29–30 — Ratio-of-Distances Loci & Exercises: Reduces linear combinations of distances from two intersecting lines to straight-line loci passing through their vertex.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Models scaling transformations in geometor/fundamentals and geometor/elements Book VI, where rational scale factors preserve exact algebraic field membership.
