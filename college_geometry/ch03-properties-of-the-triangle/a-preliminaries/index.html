Section A — Preliminaries (Harmonic Division of a Segment & Apollonian Circle)¶

Internal and external division of a segment in a given ratio (§§ 54–56, Fig. 39), mutual symmetry and metric identities of harmonic conjugates (§§ 57–66), graphical quadratic segment construction (§ 67, Figs. 40a–40b), and 3 harmonic exercises.

§ 54. Problem

Divide a given segment, AB, internally and externally in a given ratio \(p : q\).
Proposition · Commentary

§ 55. Remark I

To avoid ambiguity the statement of the problem should indicate whether the segments of AB proportional to \(p\) and \(q\) shall be adjacent to A and B, respectively, as is the case of the figure, or to B and A. Otherwise there is another pair of points, \(C', D'\), the symmetric of C, D with respect to the midpoint M of AB, which solves the problem. However, in actual applications the nature of the problem usually distinguishes between the points A, B and thus leads to only one pair of points of division.

Proposition · Commentary

§ 56. Remark II

If \(p > q\), we have \(AC > CB\) and \(AD > DB\), so that the midpoint M of AB lies outside the segment CD. The same relative position of the points M, C, D prevails, if \(p < q\).

Proposition · Commentary

§ 57. Theorem

If the points C, D divide the segment AB internally and externally in the ratio \(p : q\), the points B, A divide the segment DC internally and externally in the ratio \((p + q) : (p - q)\).
Proposition · Commentary

§ 58. Corollary

If \(AB = a\) and \(CD = b\), we have \(b = 2apq : (p^2 - q^2)\).
Proposition · Commentary

§ 59. Definitions

Instead of saying that the points C, D (§ 57) divide the segment AB internally and externally in the same ratio, we shall sometimes say that the points C, D divide the segment \(AB\) harmonically, or that the points C, D separate the points \(A, B\) harmonically, or that the points C, D are harmonic conjugates with respect to the points A, B.

Proposition · Commentary

§ 60. Problem

Given three collinear points A, B, C, construct the harmonic conjugate D of C with respect to the points A, B.
Proposition · Commentary

§ 61. Theorem

The feet of the two pairs of perpendiculars dropped upon a given line from two pairs of harmonic points are also two pairs of harmonic points.
Proposition · Commentary

§ 62. Theorem

If the points C, D divide the segment AB harmonically in the ratio \(p : q\), the midpoint O of the segment CD divides the segment AB externally in the ratio \(p^2 : q^2\).
Proposition · Commentary

§ 66. Theorem

The sum of the squares of two harmonic segments is equal to four times the square of the distance between the midpoints of these segments.
