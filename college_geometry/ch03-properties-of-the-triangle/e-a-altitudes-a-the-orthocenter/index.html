Section E.a — Altitudes: The Orthocenter¶

Chapter III: Properties of the Triangle • Articles §§ 175–182 (Folios 0113–0116, pp. 94–97)

Proves the concurrency of the three altitudes of \(\triangle ABC\) at the orthocenter \(H = X(4)\) (§§ 175–182) by embedding \(\triangle ABC\) as the complementary triangle of its anticomplementary triangle.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • § 175 — Concurrency via Anticomplementary Mediators: Parallels through \(A, B, C\) to the opposite sides form a triangle whose perpendicular bisectors (mediators) are precisely the altitudes of \(\triangle ABC\), proving concurrency in one synthetic step.

  • §§ 176–179 — Circumcircle Reflections of the Orthocenter: The reflection of \(H\) across any side \(BC\)—and across the midpoint \(A'\) of \(BC\)—lies directly on the circumcircle \((O)\)!

  • §§ 180–182 — Equal-Chord & Power Identities: Establishes \(HA \cdot HD = HB \cdot HE = HC \cdot HF\) and shows that the circumcircles of \(\triangle HBC, \triangle HCA, \triangle HAB\) all have radius \(R\) equal to that of \((ABC)\).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

In geometor/model, \(H = X(4)\) is constructed over \(\mathbb{Q}(A, B, C)\) by pure linear intersection of two perpendiculars without any square-root extension.
