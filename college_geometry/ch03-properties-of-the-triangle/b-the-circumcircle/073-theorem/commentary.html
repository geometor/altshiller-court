Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

Problem § 72 combines Chapter I’s auxiliary-triangle extension (\(AD = AC\) along \(BA\) produced, § 15) with Euclid III.36 (\(DE^2 = DA \cdot DB = b(b + c)\)), locating \(D\) at the intersection of a capable arc (\(\angle BDC = \tfrac{1}{2}A\)) and a circle concentric with \((O)\) of radius \(DO = \sqrt{R^2 + b(b + c)}\). At the bottom of the page, Theorem § 73 introduces one of the most celebrated properties of the circumcenter: the circumradius \(AO\) and altitude \(AD\) are isogonal conjugates with respect to \(\angle A\) (§ 625)!

Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

FIG. 44 is the centerpiece diagram of Section B (The Circumcircle), simultaneously illustrating §§ 73, 74, 75, 76, 78, and 85. Because \(\angle BAD = \angle KAC = 90^\circ - B\), the circumradius \(AO\) and altitude \(AD\) are symmetric across the internal bisector \(AU\) (\(\angle DAU = \angle OAU = \tfrac{1}{2}|B - C|\)), immediately yielding the classic three-cevian construction from a single vertex \((h_a, m_a, t_a)\) in § 76!
