Section B — The Circumcircle (incl. Problem of Pappus § 84)¶

Circumcircle envelope and datum (a, A, R) (§§ 68–72, Figs. 41–43), circumdiameter–altitude–bisector–median relations (§§ 73–78, Fig. 44), antipodal chord and pedal symmetry theorems (§§ 79–82, Fig. 45), the classical Problem of Pappus (§§ 83–84, Fig. 46), product of sides bc = 2Rh_a and abc = 4RS (§§ 85–86), and 35 circumcircle construction exercises.

§ 68. Theorem

If a triangle inscribed in a given circle has a constant angle, then the opposite side is tangent to a fixed circle concentric with the given circle.

§ 69. Corollary

We have the datum:

\[a,\; A,\; R.\]

§ 73. Theorem

The *angle* between the *circumdiameter* and the *altitude* issued from the same *vertex* of a *triangle* (a) is equal to the difference of the other two angles of the triangle, and (b) is bisected by the *bisector* of the angle of the triangle at the vertex considered.
Proposition · Commentary

§ 74. Corollary

In the right triangle ADU we have \(AD = h_a\), \(AU = t_a\), and angle \(DAU = \tfrac{1}{2}(B - C)\), i.e., we have the datum:

\[h_a,\quad t_a,\quad B - C.\]
Proposition · Commentary

§ 75. Theorem

The difference of the two angles which an *internal bisector* of an angle of a *triangle* makes with the *opposite side* is equal to the difference of the angles adjacent to this side.

§ 79. Theorem

The *internal* and *external* bisectors of an angle of a *triangle* pass through the ends of the *circumdiameter* which is perpendicular to the *side* opposite the *vertex* considered.
Proposition · Commentary

§ 80. Theorem

The angle which the *external bisector* makes with the *opposite side* is equal to half the difference of the two angles adjacent to this side.
Proposition · Commentary

§ 81. Corollary I

If the *tangent* at A to the *circumcircle* meets BC in T, we have TA = TU \(=\) TU'.
Proposition · Commentary

§ 82. Corollary II

The altitude AD of ABC is also an altitude of the triangle UAU'; hence:

\[t_a,\; t_a',\; h_a,\; B - C\]

constitute a datum such that any two of these four magnitudes being given, the remaining two are determined.

§ 85. Theorem

The product of two sides of a *triangle* is equal to the *altitude* to the third side multiplied by the *circumdiameter*.
Proposition · Commentary

§ 86. Corollary

The area of a triangle is equal to the product of its three sides divided by the double circumdiameter of the triangle.
