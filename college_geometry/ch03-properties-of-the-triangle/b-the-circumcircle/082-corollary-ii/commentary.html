Mathematical & Constructive Commentary¶

Articles §§ 83–84 demonstrate how Greek neusis (verging/insertion) problems reduce to Euclidean ruler-and-compass constructions when symmetry is present:

  • In § 83, because \(E\) bisects arc \(BEC\), the diameter \(EMF\) bisects chord \(BC\) perpendicularly at \(M\), making \(\triangle EUM \sim \triangle EFA\) (euclid:elements III.31). The product \(EU \cdot EA = EM \cdot EF\) reduces \(x(x + t) = 2Rm\) directly to the tangent-secant quadratic construction of § 67 (Fig. 40b).

  • § 84 (The Problem of Pappus) (pappus:collection:VII; rouche) is the symmetric case of inserting a segment of given length \(a\) between the sides of a given angle \(\angle BAC\) through a point \(U\) on its internal bisector (the general asymmetric point requires a conic/cubic neusis, whereas placement on the angle bisector makes the construction purely quadratic via § 83!).
