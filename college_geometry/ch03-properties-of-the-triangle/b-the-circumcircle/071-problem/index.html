§ 71. Problem¶

Construct a *triangle* given the circumradius, the sum of the lateral sides, and the difference of the base angles \((R,\; b + c,\; B - C)\).

Let ABC be the required triangle (Fig. 42). Let the parallel to BC through A meet the circumcircle of ABC in K. In the isosceles trapezoid ABCK we have:

\[\angle ABK = ABC - KBC = B - BKA = B - BCA = B - C.\]

The given radius \(R\) and the known angle \(ABK = B - C\) determine the segment AK (§ 69). Thus in the triangle ABK we know the base AK, the opposite angle B - C, and the sum of the sides:

\[AB + BK = AB + AC = b + c;\]

hence this triangle may be constructed (§ 17). The parallel to AK through B will meet the circumcircle of ABK in the third vertex C of the required triangle ABC.

See Commentary on § 71. Problem.
