Section E.b — Altitudes: The Orthic Triangle¶

Chapter III: Properties of the Triangle • Articles §§ 183–200 (Folios 0116–0119, pp. 97–100)

Investigates the orthic (pedal) triangle \(\triangle DEF\) formed by the feet of the three altitudes (§§ 183–200), uncovering its antiparallel sides, optical reflection law, and Fagnano's minimum-perimeter theorem.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 183–189 — Antiparallel Sides & Altitudes as Bisectors: Cyclic quadrilaterals \(BCEF, CAFD, ABDE\) render \(EF, FD, DE\) antiparallel to \(BC, CA, AB\); the altitudes of an acute \(\triangle ABC\) bisect the interior angles of \(\triangle DEF\), making \(H\) the incenter of \(\triangle DEF\)!

  • §§ 190–193 — Circumradius perpendicularity: Each circumradius \(OA, OB, OC\) is perpendicular to the corresponding orthic side \(EF, FD, DE\) (Nagel's theorem).

  • §§ 194–200 — Fagnano’s Problem (1775): Among all triangles inscribed in an acute triangle \(ABC\), the orthic triangle \(\triangle DEF\) uniquely minimizes the perimeter, equal to \(2S / R\) (Fagnano).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Illustrated symbolically in the orthic_fagnano hero model via exact Rational Trigonometry spread equality \(s(FD, DA) = s(DA, DE)\).
