Section D.b — Tritangent Circles: Tritangent Centers¶

Chapter III: Properties of the Triangle • Articles §§ 117–130 (Folios 0092–0097, pp. 73–78)

Establishes the concurrency of the internal and external angle bisectors at the incenter \(I = X(1)\) and the three excenters \(I_a, I_b, I_c\) (§§ 117–130), revealing their orthocentric duality with \(\triangle ABC\).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 117–121 — Existence of the Four Tritangent Centers: Three internal bisectors concur at \(I\), while each internal bisector concurs with two external bisectors at \(I_a, I_b, I_c\), forming an orthocentric group for which \(\triangle ABC\) is the orthic triangle!

  • §§ 122–126 — Trident (Incenter–Excenter) Lemma: If the bisector \(AII_a\) meets the circumcircle at \(M\), then \(M\) is the midpoint of \(II_a\) and \(MB = MC = MI = MI_a\).

  • §§ 127–130 — Circumcircle of the Excentral Triangle: Shows that the circumcircle of \(\triangle I_a I_b I_c\) has radius \(2R\) and is centered at the reflection of \(I\) across the circumcenter \(O\) (\(X(40)\), the Bevan point).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Demonstrates in geometor/etc how the map \(\triangle I_a I_b I_c \mapsto \triangle ABC\) interchanges excentral/orthic invariants, sending orthocenter to incenter \(X(1)\) and nine-point circle to circumcircle.
