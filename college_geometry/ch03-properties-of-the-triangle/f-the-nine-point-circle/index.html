Section F — The Nine-Point Circle (incl. Feuerbach’s Theorem § 215)¶

Chapter III: Properties of the Triangle • Articles §§ 206–219 (Folios 0122–0127, pp. 103–108)

Presents two jewels of nineteenth-century synthetic geometry (§§ 206–219): the nine-point circle (Euler, Brianchon, Poncelet) and Feuerbach's theorem (§ 215, Feuerbach 1822).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 206–210 — The Nine-Point Circle: The three side midpoints \(A', B', C'\), the three altitude feet \(D, E, F\), and the three Euler points (midpoints of \(HA, HB, HC\)) lie on a single circle of radius \(R/2\) centered at \(N = X(5)\), the midpoint of \(OH\).

  • §§ 211–214 — Homothecy from H and G: The nine-point circle is homothetic to the circumcircle from \(H\) with ratio \(+\tfrac{1}{2}\) (bisecting every segment from \(H\) to \((O)\)) and from \(G\) with ratio \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\).

  • §§ 215–219 — Feuerbach’s Theorem (1822): The nine-point circle of a triangle is internally tangent to the incircle at the Feuerbach point \(Fe = X(11)\) (\(NI = \tfrac{1}{2}R - r\)) and externally tangent to each of the three excircles (\(NI_a = \tfrac{1}{2}R + r_a\)).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Rendered with 100% exact coordinates in the euler_feuerbach codex hero model on the Heronian \(13\text{–}14\text{–}15\) triangle, where \(Fe = (1, 8)\), \(I = (1, 4)\), and \(N = (15/16, 49/16)\).
