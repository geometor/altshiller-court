Section C — Medians (Centroid, Complementary & Anticomplementary Triangles)¶

Midline theorem (§ 87) and concurrency and 2 : 1 trisection of the three medians at the centroid G = X(2) (§ 88, Fig. 47), inaugurating the synthetic theory of the complementary (medial) and anticomplementary triangles (§§ 87–110).

§ 87. Theorem

(a) The line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and is equal to one-half its length. (b) The line drawn parallel to a side of a triangle through the midpoint of another side passes through the midpoint of the third side.

Proposition · Commentary

§ 88. Theorem

The three medians of a *triangle* meet in a *point*, and each median is trisected by this point.

Proposition · Commentary

Subsequent Articles Roadmap (§§ 89–110)¶

Following the midline theorem (§ 87) and concurrency of the three medians at the centroid \(G = X(2)\) (§ 88), Section C continues across Folios 0085–0090 (pp. 66–71, §§ 89–110) to develop the homothetic pair of the complementary and anticomplementary triangles and the metric laws of medians.

  • §§ 89–95 — Complementary & Anticomplementary Triangles: The midpoint triangle \(A'B'C'\) (complementary triangle) and parallel-through-vertices triangle (anticomplementary triangle) correspond to \(\triangle ABC\) under homothecies \(\mathcal{H}(G, -\tfrac{1}{2})\) and \(\mathcal{H}(G, -2)\).

  • §§ 96–102 — Triangle of Medians & Area Ratio: Segments parallel and equal to the three medians \(m_a, m_b, m_c\) form a triangle whose area is \(\tfrac{3}{4}\) the area of \(\triangle ABC\), yielding immediate ruler-and-compass constructions from \((m_a, m_b, m_c)\).

  • §§ 103–110 — Apollonius’ Median Theorem & Leibniz Sum: Establishes Apollonius' identity \(b^2 + c^2 = 2m_a^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}a^2\), the sum of squares \(m_a^2 + m_b^2 + m_c^2 = \tfrac{3}{4}(a^2 + b^2 + c^2)\), and \(PA^2 + PB^2 + PC^2 = GA^2 + GB^2 + GC^2 + 3PG^2\).

In geometor/etc and geometor/model, the centroid \(G = X(2) = \tfrac{1}{3}(A + B + C)\) has exact rational coordinates over \(\mathbb{Q}(A, B, C)\) and anchors the \(-2 : 1\) homothecy driving the Euler line and nine-point circle.
