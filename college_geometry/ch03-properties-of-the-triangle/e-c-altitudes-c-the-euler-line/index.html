Section E.c — Altitudes: The Euler Line¶

Chapter III: Properties of the Triangle • Articles §§ 201–205 (Folios 0120–0121, pp. 101–102)

Establishes Leonhard Euler's 1765 discovery (§§ 201–205, Euler 1765) that the circumcenter \(O\), centroid \(G\), and orthocenter \(H\) of any non-equilateral triangle are collinear on the Euler line.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 201–202 — Homothetic Proof of the Euler Line: Because \(O\) is the orthocenter of the complementary triangle \(\triangle A'B'C'\) and \(\mathcal{H}(G, -2)\) maps \(\triangle A'B'C'\) onto \(\triangle ABC\), we obtain immediately \(AH = 2\,OA'\) and \(GH = -2\,GO\).

  • §§ 203–205 — Orthocenter–Circumcenter Distance & Reconstruction: Derives \(OH^2 = 9R^2 - (a^2 + b^2 + c^2)\) and solves reconstruction problems given subsets of \((O, G, H, A)\).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Forms the central spine of Kimberling’s ETC (geometor/etc): \(X(2), X(3), X(4), X(5), X(20), X(30)\) all lie on the Euler line with exact integer/rational barycentric combinations.
