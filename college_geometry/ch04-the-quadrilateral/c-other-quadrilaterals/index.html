Section C — Other Quadrilaterals (Circumscriptible & Bicentric)¶

Chapter IV: The Quadrilateral • Articles §§ 268–281 (Folios 0154–0158, pp. 135–139)

Examines quadrilaterals circumscribed about an incircle (circumscriptible) and quadrilaterals simultaneously inscribed and circumscribed (bicentric, §§ 268–281).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 268–273 — Pitot’s Theorem: A convex quadrilateral admits an inscribed circle if and only if sums of opposite sides are equal (\(AB + CD = BC + DA\)).

  • §§ 274–277 — Newton’s Line: In any circumscribed quadrilateral, the incenter lies on the straight line joining the midpoints of the two diagonals (Newton).

  • §§ 278–281 — Bicentric Quadrilaterals: Combines cyclic and tangential conditions (\(S = \sqrt{abcd}\)), perpendicularity of opposite-contact chords, and Fuss’s relation between \(R, r\), and center distance.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Bridges Euclidean circle tangency (geometor/elements Book IV) with projective Brianchon degeneracies in geometor/pappus.
