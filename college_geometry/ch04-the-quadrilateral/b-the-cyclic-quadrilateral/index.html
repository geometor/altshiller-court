Section B — The Cyclic Quadrilateral (incl. Ptolemy’s Theorem § 255 & Anticenter)¶

Chapter IV: The Quadrilateral • Articles §§ 251–267 (Folios 0146–0154, pp. 127–135)

Studies quadrilaterals inscribed in a circle (§§ 251–267), uniting Ptolemy's diagonal theorems, Brahmagupta's area and orthodiagonal theorems, and Mathot’s anticenter.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 251–257 — Ptolemy’s First & Second Theorems: In a cyclic quadrilateral \(ABCD\), \(AC \cdot BD = AB \cdot CD + BC \cdot DA\) (§ 255, Ptolemy) and \(AC / BD = (AB \cdot DA + BC \cdot CD) / (AB \cdot BC + CD \cdot DA)\), determining individual diagonal lengths directly from the four sides.

  • §§ 258–260 — The Anticenter (Mathot Point): Lines drawn through the midpoint of each side perpendicular to the opposite side concur at the anticenter, symmetric of the circumcenter across the vertex centroid!

  • §§ 261–267 — Brahmagupta’s Theorems: Establishes \(S = \sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}\) and proves that if the diagonals of a cyclic quadrilateral are perpendicular at \(P\), the line through \(P\) bisecting one side is perpendicular to the opposite side (Brahmagupta).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Ptolemy’s relation reappears under conformal inversion in Chapter IX (§ 535) and generalizes to four tangent circles in Casey's theorem (§ 471).
