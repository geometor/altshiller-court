Chapter VI: Transversals¶

Directed Segments, Stewart’s Theorem, Menelaus’ Theorem, Ceva’s Theorem, Gergonne & Nagel Points, Isotomic Conjugates, Desargues’ Theorem

Directed segments and signed ratio criteria for collinearity and concurrency (§§ 306–344, Folios 0170–0184, pp. 151–165): Euler’s four-point identity, Stewart’s theorem, Menelaus’s transversal theorem, Ceva’s concurrency theorem, Gergonne and Nagel points, and Desargues’s two-triangle theorem.

Section A — Introductory (Directed Segments & Euler’s Identity)

Introduces signed directed segments on an oriented line (§§ 306–307, Möbius, Chasles), eliminating case-splitting between internal and external points of division.

Section B — Stewart’s Theorem

Establishes Matthew Stewart's 1746 cevian theorem (§§ 308–309, Stewart 1746), expressing the length of any segment from vertex \(A\) to line \(BC\) in terms of the side lengths and base segments.

Section C — Menelaus’ Theorem

Proves Menelaus of Alexandria's Sphaerica transversal theorem and its converse (§§ 310–325), turning collinearity proofs across synthetic geometry into concise products of three signed ratios.

Section D — Ceva’s Theorem (incl. Gergonne § 331, Nagel § 333, Desargues § 343)

Presents Giovanni Ceva's 1678 concurrency theorem (§§ 326–344, Ceva 1678), pairing Menelaus collinearity with cevian concurrency and culminating in Desargues' two-triangle theorem.

See Commentary on Chapter VI: Transversals.
