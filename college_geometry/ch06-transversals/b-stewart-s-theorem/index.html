Section B — Stewart’s Theorem¶

Chapter VI: Transversals • Articles §§ 308–309 (Folios 0171–0172, pp. 152–153)

Establishes Matthew Stewart's 1746 cevian theorem (§§ 308–309, Stewart 1746), expressing the length of any segment from vertex \(A\) to line \(BC\) in terms of the side lengths and base segments.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • § 308 — Stewart’s Theorem: For any three collinear points \(B, C, D\) on the base and arbitrary apex \(A\), signed segments obey \(AB^2 \cdot CD + AC^2 \cdot DB + AD^2 \cdot BC + BC \cdot CD \cdot DB = 0\).

  • § 309 — Unified Cevian Corollaries: Specializing \(D\) to the midpoint or bisector trace recovering Apollonius’ median formula and the angle-bisector length formula in a single stroke.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

In geometor/wildberger, Stewart’s identity is a pure polynomial relation among quadrances requiring zero trigonometric functions or square roots.
