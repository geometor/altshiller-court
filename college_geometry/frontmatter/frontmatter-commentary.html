Editorial Overview of the Frontmatter (0001–0019)¶

The nineteen frontmatter folios of Nathan Altshiller-Court's College Geometry (1952 / Dover 2007) frame both the historical triumph of synthetic geometry in twentieth-century American higher education and the pedagogical discipline required to master geometric problem solving:

  • From 1925 to 1952 (`Preface`, pp. vii–viii): Between the 1925 Johnson Publishing first edition and the 1952 Barnes & Noble second edition, college geometry evolved from an experimental elective into a staple course for mathematics majors and prospective teachers. Altshiller-Court rewrote the entire treatise, inserted Chapter IV (The Quadrilateral), expanded Chapter X (Recent Geometry of the Triangle), and moved harmonic division forward into Chapter II/III.

  • Pagination Offset Contract: Folios 0001–0019 carry cover and Roman numerals [iii]–[xx] (including blank versos 0005, 0009, 0015, 0019), establishing the exact body formula PDF Folio = Printed Arabic Page + 19 from Folio 0020 (p. 1) to Folio 0332 (p. 313).

  • Problem-Solving Heuristic (`To the Student`, pp. xvii–xix): Altshiller-Court’s insistence on maintaining bidirectional marginal links to Euclid's Elements, memorizing exact definitions and theorems as active deductive tools, restating problems from memory, and sketching diagnostic figures directly parallels George Pólya’s How to Solve It (1945) and motivates the GEOMETOR Digital Scriptorium.
