The Simson Line (Wallace-Simson Theorems & Exercises)¶

Chapter V: The Simson Line • Articles §§ 282–305 (Folios 0159–0169, pp. 140–150)

Develops the complete synthetic theory of the Wallace–Simson line (§§ 282–305, Folios 0159–0169, pp. 140–150), discovered by William Wallace in 1799 and traditionally named after Robert Simson.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 282–287 — Wallace–Simson Collinearity Criterion: The feet \(X, Y, Z\) of the perpendiculars dropped from a point \(P\) onto the sidelines \(BC, CA, AB\) of \(\triangle ABC\) are collinear if and only if \(P\) lies on the circumcircle of \(\triangle ABC\).

  • §§ 288–297 — Angular Rotation & Perpendicular Simson Lines: As \(P\) traverses the circumcircle, its Simson line rotates at half the angular rate in the opposite sense; the Simson lines of two diametrically opposite points \(P, P'\) are mutually perpendicular.

  • §§ 298–305 — Bisection of PH on the Nine-Point Circle: The Simson line of \(P\) bisects the segment \(PH\) joining \(P\) to the orthocenter \(H\) at a point \(M\) on the nine-point circle, and two diametral Simson lines intersect right on the nine-point circle (Steiner's three-cusped hypocycloid envelope).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Verified symbolically in the simson_line codex hero model (\(P = (4, 12)\) on the \(13\text{–}14\text{–}15\) circumcircle), proving collinearity of \(X, Y, Z, M\) and concyclicity of \(M\) with the nine-point circle.
