Theory and Applications of Circular Inversion¶

Chapter IX: Inversion • Articles §§ 518–546 (Folios 0249–0262, pp. 230–243)

Develops conformal transformation by reciprocal radii—circular inversion \(\mathcal{I}(O, k)\) (§§ 518–546, Folios 0249–0262, pp. 230–243)—converting intractable circle-tangency configurations into elementary rectilinear geometry.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 518–531 — Images of Lines, Circles & Conformal Angle Preservation: Lines and circles through \(O\) invert into lines; lines and circles not through \(O\) invert into circles; angles between intersecting curves are preserved in magnitude; metric law \(A'B' = |k| \cdot AB / (OA \cdot OB)\).

  • §§ 532–534 — Peaucellier’s Inversor Linkage (§ 533): Proves that Peaucellier's 1864 seven-bar rhombic cell realizes exact mathematical inversion and converts circular arc motion into a rigorous straight line without any reference straightedge!

  • §§ 535–546 — Ptolemy, Steiner Porism & Feuerbach by Inversion (§ 540): Proves Ptolemy’s theorem and inequality by inverting from a vertex, resolves Steiner’s porismatic ring of tangent circles via concentric inversion, and proves Feuerbach's theorem in a single paragraph by inverting from the midpoint of the interior common tangent to \((I)\) and \((I_a)\)!

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Modeled symbolically in the circular_inversion hero diagram (\(OP \cdot OP' = 36\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\)) and reproducible algebraically in geometor/model.
