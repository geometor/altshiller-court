Section D — Isogonal Lines (Isogonal Conjugates & Pedal Triangles)¶

Chapter X: Recent Geometry of the Triangle • Articles §§ 625–648 (Folios 0286–0293, pp. 267–274)

Develops the quadratic Cremona transformation of isogonal conjugation with respect to \(\triangle ABC\) (§§ 625–648), pairing every interior/exterior point \(P\) with its reflection-in-bisectors conjugate \(P'\).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 625–634 — Existence & Classical Conjugate Pairs: Reflections of concurrent cevians \(AP, BP, CP\) across the internal angle bisectors concur at \(P'\); incenter/excenters are self-conjugate, \((O, H)\) are isogonal conjugates, and \((G, K)\) are isogonal conjugates!

  • §§ 635–643 — Shared Pedal Circle Theorem: The six pedal projections of an isogonal pair \(P, P'\) onto the three sides lie on a single pedal circle centered at the midpoint of \(PP'\) (specializing for \((O, H)\) right back to the nine-point circle!).

  • §§ 644–648 — Circumcircle Degeneracy: Points on the circumcircle have isogonal conjugates at infinity in the direction perpendicular to their Simson lines.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Corresponds to reciprocal trilinear coordinates \((u : v : w) \mapsto (1/u : 1/v : 1/w)\) across geometor/etc.
