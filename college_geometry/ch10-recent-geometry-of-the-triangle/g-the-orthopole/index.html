Section G — The Orthopole¶

Chapter X: Recent Geometry of the Triangle • Articles §§ 690–699 (Folios 0306–0310, pp. 287–291)

Culminates the entire 699-article treatise with Joseph Neuberg's theory of the orthopole \(M\) of a line \(m\) with respect to \(\triangle ABC\) (§§ 690–699, Gallatly 1913).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 690–694 — Existence & Concurrency: Drop perpendiculars \(AA_1, BB_1, CC_1\) from the vertices of \(\triangle ABC\) onto any line \(m\); the perpendiculars from \(A_1, B_1, C_1\) onto the opposite sides \(BC, CA, AB\) concur at the orthopole \(M\) of \(m\)!

  • §§ 695–699 — Circumdiameter, Simson & Pedal-Circle Laws: If \(m\) passes through the circumcenter \(O\), its orthopole lies on the nine-point circle (and equals the Simson-line intersection of the diameter endpoints!); if \(m\) cuts \((O)\) in \(P, Q\), then \(M\) is the intersection of the Simson lines of \(P\) and \(Q\) (Soons, Neuberg, Lemoyne)!

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Synthesizes orthocenter, nine-point, Simson-line, and pedal-circle geometry into one unified incidence theorem verifiable in geometor/model.

See Commentary on Chapter X: Recent Geometry of the Triangle and 334-Folio Archival Scan Inventory.
