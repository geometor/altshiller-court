Chapter VIII: Circles¶

Inverse Points, Orthogonal Circles, Poles and Polars, Centers of Similitude, Power of a Point, Radical Axis, Coaxal Circles, Casey’s Theorem, Problem of Apollonius

Comprehensive geometry of one, two, and three circles (§§ 358–517, Folios 0191–0248, pp. 172–229): inverse points, orthogonal circles, poles and polars, Monge’s similitude axes, power and radical axis, coaxal pencils, Casey’s theorem, and Gergonne’s solution to the Problem of Apollonius.

Section A — Inverse Points

Defines inverse points P, P’ with respect to a reference circle (O, R) (§§ 358–362) via collinearity on a ray from O and OP cdot OP’ = R^2.

Section B — Orthogonal Circles

Develops the geometry of orthogonal circles (§§ 363–372)—pairs of circles whose tangents (and radii) at their points of intersection meet at right angles.

Section C — Poles and Polars

Presents the projective-metric duality of poles and polars with respect to a circle (O, R) (§§ 373–393, Apollonius , Desargues , La Hire , Poncelet ).

Section D — Centers of Similitude (incl. Monge’s Circle Theorem § 405)

Extends Chapter II’s centers of similitude to systems of two and three circles (§§ 394–409), culminating in Gaspard Monge’s theorem on axes of similitude.

Section E — Power of a Point with Respect to a Circle

Formalizes Jakob Steiner’s algebraic-geometric invariant (§§ 410–420): the power of a point P with respect to circle (O, R).

Section F — Radical Axis of Two Circles

Constructs and characterizes the radical axis of two non-concentric circles (§§ 421–439, Gaultier 1813, Steiner ): the straight-line locus of points having equal powers with respect to both circles.

Section G — Coaxal Circles (incl. Poncelet’s Theorems & Casey’s Theorem § 471)

Develops the theory of coaxal pencils of circles (§§ 440–490), Poncelet’s limiting points, conjugate orthogonal pencils, and John Casey’s generalized Ptolemy theorem (§ 471).

Section H — Three Circles (Radical Center & Circles of Antisimilitude)

Analyzes systems of three circles (§§ 491–505): the concurrency of their three pairwise radical axes at the radical center and their orthogonal circle.

Section I — The Problem of Apollonius (Ten Problems I–X & Gergonne’s Solution)

Crowns Chapter VIII with the Problem of Apollonius (§§ 506–517): to construct a circle tangent to three given circles (or any combination of points P, lines L, and circles C).

See Commentary on Chapter VIII: Circles.
