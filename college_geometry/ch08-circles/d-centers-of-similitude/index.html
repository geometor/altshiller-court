Section D — Centers of Similitude (incl. Monge’s Circle Theorem § 405)¶

Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 394–409 (Folios 0203–0209, pp. 184–190)

Extends Chapter II’s centers of similitude to systems of two and three circles (§§ 394–409), culminating in Gaspard Monge's theorem on axes of similitude.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 394–404 — Homologous & Antihomologous Points: Pairs of antihomologous points on two circles are concyclic and yield constant secant products from each center of similitude, governing common tangent constructions.

  • §§ 405–409 — Monge’s Circle Theorem (§ 405): Given three unequal circles, their six centers of similitude lie by threes on four straight lines (axes of similitude): one external axis containing all three external centers, and three mixed axes containing one external and two internal centers (Monge).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

The four axes of similitude constructed here form the essential first step of Gergonne's master solution to the Problem of Apollonius (§ 517).
