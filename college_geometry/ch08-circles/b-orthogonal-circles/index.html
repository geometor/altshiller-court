Section B — Orthogonal Circles¶

Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 363–372 (Folios 0193–0196, pp. 174–177)

Develops the geometry of orthogonal circles (§§ 363–372)—pairs of circles whose tangents (and radii) at their points of intersection meet at right angles.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 363–367 — Metric & Tangent Criteria: Circles \((O, R)\) and \((O', R')\) are orthogonal if and only if \(OO'^2 = R^2 + R'^2\), or equivalently the radius of one to an intersection point is tangent to the other.

  • §§ 368–372 — Fundamental Inversion-Orthogonality Theorem: A circle cuts \((O, R)\) orthogonally if and only if every diameter of \((O, R)\) meeting it is divided harmonically—i.e., if and only if it passes through the inverse \(P'\) of every point \(P\) it contains!

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Verified symbolically in the pole_polar_coaxal hero figure with exact Pythagorean radii \(6, 8\) on center distance \(10\).
