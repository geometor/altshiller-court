Section C — Poles and Polars¶

Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 373–393 (Folios 0196–0203, pp. 177–184)

Presents the projective-metric duality of poles and polars with respect to a circle \((O, R)\) (§§ 373–393, Apollonius, Desargues, La Hire, Poncelet).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 373–378 — Polar Definition & Chord of Contact: Defines the polar of \(P\) as the perpendicular to \(OP\) through its inverse point \(P'\); for external \(P\), it coincides with the chord of contact \(T_1 T_2\), and divides every secant chord through \(P\) harmonically.

  • §§ 379–385 — La Hire’s Reciprocity Theorem: Point \(Q\) lies on the polar of \(P\) if and only if \(P\) lies on the polar of \(Q\) (La Hire, Sectiones Conicae), translating collinear ranges into concurrent pencils.

  • §§ 386–393 — Conjugate (Self-Polar) Triangles & Salmon’s Theorem: Shows that the orthocenter of an obtuse self-polar triangle is the circle’s center \(O\), and relates pole distances to polar distances (Salmon’s proportional identity).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Bridges Euclidean circle polarity in geometor/model with general conic polarity in geometor/pappus.
