Section G — Coaxal Circles (incl. Poncelet’s Theorems & Casey’s Theorem § 471)¶

Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 440–490 (Folios 0220–0236, pp. 201–217)

Develops the theory of coaxal pencils of circles (§§ 440–490), Poncelet's limiting points, conjugate orthogonal pencils, and John Casey's generalized Ptolemy theorem (§ 471).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities¶

  • §§ 440–458 — Intersecting, Tangent & Non-Intersecting Pencils: Classifies coaxal systems sharing a common radical axis, identifies Poncelet's point-circles (limiting points \(L, L'\)), and proves that two conjugate orthogonal coaxal pencils swap base points and limiting points!

  • §§ 459–470 — Poncelet’s Triangle-Pencil Theorems: Studies variable triangles inscribed in one circle whose sides touch circles of a coaxal pencil.

  • §§ 471–490 — Casey’s Theorem (§ 471): Four circles tangent to a fifth circle (or line) satisfy \(t_{12} t_{34} \pm t_{13} t_{24} \pm t_{14} t_{23} = 0\) among their common tangent lengths (Casey, Sequel to Euclid), providing an algebraic proof of Feuerbach’s theorem!

Constructive & Symbolic Connections¶

Connects projective pencils of conics in geometor/pappus with Feuerbach tangency verification in geometor/etc.
