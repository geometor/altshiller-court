.. _person-altshiller-court:

Altshiller-Court, Nathan (1881–1968)
------------------------------------

* **Role**: Mathematician & Geometer, University of Oklahoma
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 4, § 11, § 219, § 324, § 360, § 488, § 502, § 503
* **Principal Works**: *College Geometry (1925; 2nd ed. 1952)*; *Modern Pure Solid Geometry (1935)*; *Mathematics in Fun and in Earnest (1958)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0001 (p. [cover]) <frontmatter-01-cover>` · :ref:`f. 0002 (p. [iii]) <frontmatter-02-title-and-dedication>` · :ref:`f. 0003 (p. [iv]) <frontmatter-02-title-and-dedication>` · :ref:`f. 0007 (p. viii) <frontmatter-03-preface>` · :ref:`f. 0008 (p. ix) <frontmatter-03-preface>` · :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Born in Warsaw on January 22, 1881, **Nathan Altshiller-Court** pursued his mathematical studies in Belgium at the **University of Ghent**, earning his *Doctor of Science* in 1911 within the vibrant Franco-Belgian synthetic geometry tradition shaped by **Paul Mansion** and **Joseph Neuberg** (*Mathesis*). Emigrating to the United States, he studied at Columbia University and taught briefly at the University of Washington and the University of Colorado before joining the **University of Oklahoma** in Norman in 1916, where he served as Professor of Mathematics until his retirement in 1951.

Across half a century of scholarship, Altshiller-Court authored three enduring works:

1. *College Geometry: A Second Course in Plane Geometry for Colleges and Normal Schools* (Richmond: Johnson Publishing Co., 1925), thoroughly rewritten and expanded as *College Geometry: An Introduction to the Modern Geometry of the Triangle and the Circle* (New York: Barnes & Noble, 1952; Dover republication, 2007).
2. *Modern Pure Solid Geometry* (New York: Macmillan, 1935; 2nd ed. Chelsea, 1964), extending synthetic methods to the tetrahedron and sphere.
3. *Mathematics in Fun and in Earnest* (New York: Dial Press, 1958), philosophical and historical essays on mathematical invention and intuition.

Cited Geometers, Philosophers & Scholars
----------------------------------------

.. _person-apollonius:

Apollonius of Perga (c. 262 – c. 190 BC)
----------------------------------------

* **Role**: Hellenistic Geometer ('The Great Geometer')
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 11, § 61, § 506, § 516, § 597, § 599
* **Principal Works**: *Conics*; *Plane Loci (τόποι ἐπίπεδοι)*; *Tangencies (ἐπαφαί)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0034 (p. 15) <ch01-locus-11>` · :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Author of the Conics and lost treatises Plane Loci (reconstructed by Robert Simson, 1749) and Tangencies (On Contacts, reconstructed by Viète, 1600). Namesake of the Apollonian circle of constant distance ratio (§ 11, Locus 11), the three Apollonian circles of a triangle (Ch. X, § 597), and the Problem of Apollonius on circles tangent to three given circles (Ch. VIII, §§ 506–517).

.. _person-archimedes:

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287 – 212 BC)
----------------------------------------

* **Role**: Greek Mathematician, Physicist & Engineer
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 141, § 469
* **Principal Works**: *Book of Lemmas (Liber Assumptorum)*; *Measurement of a Circle*; *On Spirals*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Greatest mathematician of antiquity, cited by Altshiller-Court for theorems on chords, circles, the arbelos (shoemaker's knife), and precursors to harmonic and metric relations.

Brahmagupta (598 – c. 668)

* **Role**: Indian Mathematician & Astronomer

* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

.. _person-brianchon:

Brianchon, Charles Julien (1783–1864)
-------------------------------------

* **Role**: French Projective Geometer
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 207, § 224, § 381, § 449
* **Principal Works**: *Recherches sur la détermination d'une hyperbole équilatère (with Poncelet, 1820–1821)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Student at the École Polytechnique under Monge. With Poncelet (1821), published the first complete proof of the Nine-Point Circle theorem and discovered Brianchon's hexagram theorem.

.. _person-brocard:

Brocard, Henri (1845–1922)
--------------------------

* **Role**: French Meteorologist & Army Officer-Geometer
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 606, § 618, § 650, § 654, § 658, § 661, § 665, § 667, § 668, § 669, § 670, § 672, § 673, § 677
* **Principal Works**: *Étude d'un nouveau cercle du plan du triangle (1881)*; *Notes de bibliographie des courbes géométriques*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Co-founder (with Lemoine and Neuberg) of the late-19th-century revival of triangle geometry. Investigated the Brocard points Ω, Ω', Brocard angle ω, Brocard circle, and Brocard triangles (Ch. X, §§ 649–683).

.. _person-carnot:

Carnot, Lazare Nicolas Marguerite (1753–1823)
---------------------------------------------

* **Role**: French Mathematician & Statesman ('Organizer of Victory')
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 133, § 146, § 175, § 185, § 224, § 310, § 314
* **Principal Works**: *Géométrie de position (1803)*; *Essai sur la théorie des transversales (1806)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Pioneered the systematic use of signed geometric quantities and transversals in Géométrie de position (1803); proved Carnot's Theorem relating circumradius, inradius, and signed distances from circumcenter to triangle sides (§ 146).

.. _person-casey:

Casey, John (1820–1891)
-----------------------

* **Role**: Irish Geometer, Catholic University of Ireland
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 362, § 471, § 475, § 587, § 661
* **Principal Works**: *A Sequel to the First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid (1881; 6th ed. 1892)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Author of A Sequel to the First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid (1881), one of the primary English precursors to College Geometry. Formulated Casey's Theorem (§ 471), generalizing Ptolemy's theorem to four circles tangent to a fifth circle, leading to an elegant proof of Feuerbach's theorem.

.. _person-castillon:

Castillon, Johann (Salvemini, Giovanni Francesco) (1709–1791)
-------------------------------------------------------------

* **Role**: Italian-Swiss Geometer, Berlin Academy
* **Articles in College Geometry**: Frontmatter / Apparatus
* **Principal Works**: *Sur un problème de géométrie plane (Nouveaux Mémoires de l'Académie de Berlin, 1776)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0027 (p. 8) <ch01-art-009>`

Solved in 1776 the celebrated Cramer-Castillon Problem of inscribing in a given circle a triangle whose sides pass through three given points—a projective-constructive counterpart to Chapter I's incidence problems (§ 9, § 14).

.. _person-euclid:

Euclid of Alexandria (fl. c. 300 BC)

.. _person-euler:

Euler, Leonhard (1707–1783)
---------------------------

* **Role**: Swiss Mathematician & Natural Philosopher
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 93, § 134, § 152, § 175, § 201, § 207, § 246, § 255, § 307
* **Principal Works**: *Solutio facilis problematum quorumdam geometricorum difficillimorum (1765)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Initiated modern triangle geometry in his 1765 St. Petersburg memoir: proved the collinearity of the orthocenter H, centroid G, and circumcenter O on the Euler line (§ 201), established OI² = R² - 2Rr (§ 152), Euler's collinear four-point identity (§ 307), and quadrilateral median relations (§ 246).

* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Posed and solved Fagnano's problem (1775): among all triangles inscribed in an acute triangle, the orthic triangle has minimum perimeter (§ 194).

.. _person-feuerbach:

Feuerbach, Karl Wilhelm (1800–1834)
-----------------------------------

* **Role**: German Geometer
* **Articles in College Geometry**: § 134, § 136, § 207, § 215, § 224, § 540
* **Principal Works**: *Eigenschaften einiger merkwürdigen Punkte des geradlinigen Dreiecks (1822)*
* **Edition Loci**: :ref:`f. 0326 (p. 307) <historical-and-bibliographical-notes-names-list-of-names>`

Published in 1822 at age twenty-two one of the crown jewels of elementary geometry: Feuerbach's Theorem (§ 215, § 540), proving that the nine-point circle of a triangle is internally tangent to the incircle (at the Feuerbach point X(11)) and externally tangent to the three excircles.

.. _person-gergonne:

Gergonne, Joseph Diaz (1771–1859)
