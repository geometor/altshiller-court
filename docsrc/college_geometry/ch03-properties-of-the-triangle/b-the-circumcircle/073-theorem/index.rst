§ 73. Theorem
=============

.. folio:: 0078
   :page: 59

.. enunciation::

   *The* :g:`*angle* <0079.g1:< D A K )>` *between the* :g:`*circumdiameter* <0079.g1:/ A K />` *and the* :g:`*altitude* <0079.g1:/ A D />` *issued from the same* :g:`*vertex* <0079.g1:A>` *of a* :g:`*triangle* <0079.g1:< A B C >>` *(a) is equal to the difference of the other two angles of the triangle, and (b) is bisected by the* :g:`*bisector* <0079.g1:/ A U />` *of the angle of the triangle at the vertex considered.*

.. folio:: 0079
   :page: 60

\(a) Let :g:`AD <0079.g1:/ A D />` be the altitude and :g:`AK <0079.g1:/ A K />` the circumdiameter issued from the vertex :g:`A <0079.g1:A>` (Fig. 44). The angles :g:`B <0079.g1:< A B D )>` and :g:`K <0079.g1:< A K C )>` are equal since they subtend the same :g:`chord <0079.g1:/ A C />` of the :term:`circumcircle <circumcircle>`; hence we have from the right triangles :g:`ABD <0079.g1:< A B D >>`, :g:`ACK <0079.g1:< A C K >>`:

.. picture:: 0079.g1.svg
   :alt: Fig. 44 — Circumdiameter AK through circumcenter O, altitude AD, bisector AU, and median AA' of triangle ABC
   :caption: FIG. 44

.. math::

   \angle BAD = \angle KAC = 90^\circ - B,

and:

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \angle DAK = A - 2(90^\circ - B) = A + 2B - 180^\circ &= \\
   A + 2B - A - B - C &= B - C.
   \end{aligned}

The student may verify that the proposition holds when one of the angles :math:`B, C` is obtuse.

\(b) Let :g:`AU <0079.g1:/ A U />` be the bisector of the angle :math:`A`. Since :eq:`∠BAD = CAO <0079.g1:< B A D ) = 0079.g1:< K A C )>`, we have angle :eq:`DAU = OAU <0079.g1:< D A U ) = 0079.g1:< O A U )>`.
