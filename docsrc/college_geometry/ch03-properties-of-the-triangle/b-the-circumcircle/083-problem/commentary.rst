Mathematical & Constructive Commentary
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Articles §§ 83–84 demonstrate how Greek *neusis* (verging/insertion) problems reduce to Euclidean ruler-and-compass constructions when symmetry is present:

- In **§ 83**, because :math:`E` bisects arc :math:`BEC`, the diameter :math:`EMF` bisects chord :math:`BC` perpendicularly at :math:`M`, making :math:`\triangle EUM \sim \triangle EFA` (:cite:`euclid:elements` III.31). The product :math:`EU \cdot EA = EM \cdot EF` reduces :math:`x(x + t) = 2Rm` directly to the tangent-secant quadratic construction of **§ 67** (Fig. 40b).
- **§ 84 (The Problem of Pappus)** (:cite:`pappus:collection:VII`; :cite:`rouche`) is the symmetric case of inserting a segment of given length :math:`a` between the sides of a given angle :math:`\angle BAC` through a point :math:`U` on its internal bisector (the general asymmetric point requires a conic/cubic *neusis*, whereas placement on the angle bisector makes the construction purely quadratic via § 83!).
