Section C — Menelaus' Theorem
=============================

.. rubric:: Chapter VI: Transversals • Articles §§ 310–325 (Folios 0172–0177, pp. 153–158)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Triangle ABC with concurrent cevians AX, BY, CZ at Gergonne point Ge and Menelaus transversal UZY
   :caption: Ceva Concurrency at Gergonne Point X(7) and Harmonic Menelaus Transversal

Proves :person:`Menelaus of Alexandria's <menelaus>` *Sphaerica* transversal theorem and its converse (**§§ 310–325**), turning collinearity proofs across synthetic geometry into concise products of three signed ratios.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 310–313 — Menelaus' Theorem & Converse**: Points :math:`L, M, N` on sidelines :math:`BC, CA, AB` are collinear if and only if :math:`\frac{BL}{LC} \cdot \frac{CM}{MA} \cdot \frac{AN}{NB} = -1`.
* **§§ 314–320 — Bisector Traces & Tangential Axis**: Immediate proofs that the three external angle-bisector feet (and two internal with one external) are collinear, and that tangents to the circumcircle at :math:`A, B, C` meet the opposite sides in three collinear points (the Lemoine axis).
* **§§ 321–325 — Similitude Axes & Pascal's Hexagon**: Proves :person:`Monge's <monge>` theorem on the collinearity of external centers of similitude of three circles and Pascal's theorem for a hexagon inscribed in a circle.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

Directly powers projective collinearity verification in ``geometor/pappus`` (Pappus's and Pascal's hexagon theorems).
