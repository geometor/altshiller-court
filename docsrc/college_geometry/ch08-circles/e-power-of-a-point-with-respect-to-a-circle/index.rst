Section E — Power of a Point with Respect to a Circle
=====================================================

.. rubric:: Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 410–420 (Folios 0209–0213, pp. 190–194)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Reference circle O and orthogonal circle P intersecting perpendicularly at T1, T2 with polar chord T1 T2 through inverse point P'
   :caption: Inverse Points P, P', Polar Line T₁T₂, and Orthogonal Circle (P, PT₁)

Formalizes :person:`Jakob Steiner's <steiner>` algebraic-geometric invariant (**§§ 410–420**): the :term:`power of a point <power-of-a-point>` :math:`P` with respect to circle :math:`(O, R)`.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 410–415 — Signed Secant Product**: For any line through :math:`P` meeting :math:`(O, R)` in :math:`A, B`, the signed product :math:`PA \cdot PB = PO^2 - R^2` depends solely on :math:`P` (:cite:`Euclid III.35–36 <euclid:elements>`): positive outside (equal to :math:`PT^2`), zero on, and negative inside (:math:`-\text{half-min-chord}^2`).
* **§§ 416–420 — Orthogonal Circle Power Criterion**: The power of the center of one circle with respect to a second circle equals the square of its own radius if and only if the two circles are :term:`orthogonal <orthogonal-circles>`.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

Represented in ``geometor/model`` and ``geometor/wildberger`` as the exact rational polynomial :math:`Q(P, O) - R^2`.
