Section A — Inverse Points
==========================

.. rubric:: Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 358–362 (Folios 0191–0193, pp. 172–174)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Reference circle O and orthogonal circle P intersecting perpendicularly at T1, T2 with polar chord T1 T2 through inverse point P'
   :caption: Inverse Points P, P', Polar Line T₁T₂, and Orthogonal Circle (P, PT₁)

Defines :term:`inverse points <inverse-points>` :math:`P, P'` with respect to a reference circle :math:`(O, R)` (**§§ 358–362**) via collinearity on a ray from :math:`O` and :math:`OP \cdot OP' = R^2`.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 358–360 — Harmonic Diameter Separation**: Two points :math:`P, P'` on a diameter line are inverse with respect to :math:`(O, R)` if and only if they divide the endpoints of that diameter :term:`harmonically <harmonic-division>` (§ 63).
* **§§ 361–362 — Ruler-and-Compass Construction & Ratio Property**: Constructs :math:`P'` via right-triangle chords or tangent secants, and shows that :math:`(O, R)` is an :term:`Apollonian circle <apollonian-circle>` for every pair of inverse points :math:`MP / MP' = OP / R`.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

Establishes the point-pair foundation for poles/polars (§ 373) and conformal :term:`inversion <inversion>` (Chapter IX).
