Section C — Poles and Polars
============================

.. rubric:: Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 373–393 (Folios 0196–0203, pp. 177–184)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Reference circle O and orthogonal circle P intersecting perpendicularly at T1, T2 with polar chord T1 T2 through inverse point P'
   :caption: Inverse Points P, P', Polar Line T₁T₂, and Orthogonal Circle (P, PT₁)

Presents the projective-metric duality of :term:`poles and polars <pole-and-polar>` with respect to a circle :math:`(O, R)` (**§§ 373–393**, :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>`, :person:`Desargues <desargues>`, :person:`La Hire <hire>`, :person:`Poncelet <poncelet>`).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 373–378 — Polar Definition & Chord of Contact**: Defines the polar of :math:`P` as the perpendicular to :math:`OP` through its inverse point :math:`P'`; for external :math:`P`, it coincides with the chord of contact :math:`T_1 T_2`, and divides every secant chord through :math:`P` harmonically.
* **§§ 379–385 — La Hire's Reciprocity Theorem**: Point :math:`Q` lies on the polar of :math:`P` if and only if :math:`P` lies on the polar of :math:`Q` (:person:`La Hire <hire>`, :cite:`Sectiones Conicae <lhr>`), translating collinear ranges into concurrent pencils.
* **§§ 386–393 — Conjugate (Self-Polar) Triangles & Salmon's Theorem**: Shows that the orthocenter of an obtuse self-polar triangle is the circle's center :math:`O`, and relates pole distances to polar distances (Salmon's proportional identity).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

Bridges Euclidean circle polarity in ``geometor/model`` with general conic polarity in ``geometor/pappus``.
