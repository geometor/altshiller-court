Section D — Centers of Similitude (incl. Monge's Circle Theorem § 405)
======================================================================

.. rubric:: Chapter VIII: Circles • Articles §§ 394–409 (Folios 0203–0209, pp. 184–190)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Two unequal circles O, O' with parallel radii, external center of similitude S, internal center S', and dashed circle of similitude
   :caption: Direct and Inverse Centers of Similitude S, S' and Circle of Similitude

Extends Chapter II's :term:`centers of similitude <centers-of-similitude>` to systems of two and three circles (**§§ 394–409**), culminating in :person:`Gaspard Monge's <monge>` theorem on axes of similitude.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 394–404 — Homologous & Antihomologous Points**: Pairs of antihomologous points on two circles are concyclic and yield constant secant products from each center of similitude, governing common tangent constructions.
* **§§ 405–409 — Monge's Circle Theorem (§ 405)**: Given three unequal circles, their six centers of similitude lie by threes on **four straight lines** (*axes of similitude*): one external axis containing all three external centers, and three mixed axes containing one external and two internal centers (:person:`Monge <monge>`).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

The four axes of similitude constructed here form the essential first step of :person:`Gergonne's <gergonne>` master solution to the :term:`Problem of Apollonius <problem-of-apollonius>` (§ 517).
