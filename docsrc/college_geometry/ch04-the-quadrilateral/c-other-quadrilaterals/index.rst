Section C — Other Quadrilaterals (Circumscriptible & Bicentric)
===============================================================

.. rubric:: Chapter IV: The Quadrilateral • Articles §§ 268–281 (Folios 0154–0158, pp. 135–139)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Cyclic quadrilateral ABCD inscribed in circle O with diagonals AC, BD and Varignon midpoint parallelogram EFGH

Examines quadrilaterals circumscribed about an incircle (*circumscriptible*) and quadrilaterals simultaneously inscribed and circumscribed (*bicentric*, **§§ 268–281**).

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 268–273 — Pitot's Theorem**: A convex quadrilateral admits an inscribed circle if and only if sums of opposite sides are equal (:math:`AB + CD = BC + DA`).
* **§§ 274–277 — Newton's Line**: In any circumscribed quadrilateral, the incenter lies on the straight line joining the midpoints of the two diagonals (:person:`Newton <newton>`).
* **§§ 278–281 — Bicentric Quadrilaterals**: Combines cyclic and tangential conditions (:math:`S = \sqrt{abcd}`), perpendicularity of opposite-contact chords, and Fuss's relation between :math:`R, r`, and center distance.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

Bridges Euclidean circle tangency (``geometor/elements`` Book IV) with projective Brianchon degeneracies in ``geometor/pappus``.
