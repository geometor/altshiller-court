Section B — The Cyclic Quadrilateral (incl. Ptolemy's Theorem § 255 & Anticenter)
=================================================================================

.. rubric:: Chapter IV: The Quadrilateral • Articles §§ 251–267 (Folios 0146–0154, pp. 127–135)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Cyclic quadrilateral ABCD inscribed in circle O with diagonals AC, BD and Varignon midpoint parallelogram EFGH
   :caption: Cyclic Quadrilateral ABCD, Ptolemy Diagonals AC, BD, and Varignon Parallelogram EFGH

Studies quadrilaterals inscribed in a circle (**§§ 251–267**), uniting :person:`Ptolemy's <ptolemy>` diagonal theorems, :person:`Brahmagupta's <brahmagupta>` area and orthodiagonal theorems, and Mathot's :term:`anticenter <anticenter>`.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 251–257 — Ptolemy's First & Second Theorems**: In a cyclic quadrilateral :math:`ABCD`, :math:`AC \cdot BD = AB \cdot CD + BC \cdot DA` (**§ 255**, :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`) and :math:`AC / BD = (AB \cdot DA + BC \cdot CD) / (AB \cdot BC + CD \cdot DA)`, determining individual diagonal lengths directly from the four sides.
* **§§ 258–260 — The Anticenter (Mathot Point)**: Lines drawn through the midpoint of each side perpendicular to the *opposite* side concur at the :term:`anticenter <anticenter>`, symmetric of the circumcenter across the vertex centroid!
* **§§ 261–267 — Brahmagupta's Theorems**: Establishes :math:`S = \sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}` and proves that if the diagonals of a cyclic quadrilateral are perpendicular at :math:`P`, the line through :math:`P` bisecting one side is perpendicular to the opposite side (:person:`Brahmagupta <brahmagupta>`).

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

Ptolemy's relation reappears under conformal :term:`inversion <inversion>` in Chapter IX (§ 535) and generalizes to four tangent circles in :person:`Casey's <casey>` theorem (§ 471).
