Mathematical & Constructive Commentary
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Unlike § 17 (where :math:`\angle D = \frac{1}{2}A < 90^\circ` lies opposite the shorter side :math:`a < b + c` and admits up to two solutions), in § 18 the obtuse angle :math:`\angle BDC = 90^\circ + \frac{1}{2}A` lies opposite the *longer* side :math:`BC = a > b - c = CD`, guaranteeing a unique auxiliary triangle :math:`\triangle BCD` whenever the triangle inequality :math:`a > b - c` holds. Notice how § 18 folds :math:`AB` *inward* onto :math:`AC` (giving altitude :math:`h_b` to :math:`CD`), whereas § 19 extends :math:`AB` *outward* beyond :math:`B` to :math:`E` so :math:`AE = AC` (giving altitude :math:`h_c` to :math:`BE`).

Mathematical & Constructive Commentary
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In ``FIG. 20`` (§ 19), because :math:`AE = AC = b` in isosceles :math:`\triangle ACE`, the base angles satisfy :math:`\angle AEC = \angle ACE = 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2}A`, whence :math:`\angle BCE = \angle ACE - C = 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2}A - C = \frac{1}{2}(B - C)` while :math:`\angle CBE = 180^\circ - B`, complementing the dual auxiliary triangle :math:`\triangle BCD` of § 18. Note carefully that when § 18 and § 19 become separate web pages in the item-per-page codex, any ``0043.g1`` reference inside § 19's prose would point to a figure on another page unless omitted or scoped cleanly—let us keep § 19's ``:g:`` roles strictly targeting ``0044.g1`` (its own co-located ``FIG. 20``)!
