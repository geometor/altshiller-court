Section B.c — Lemoine Geometry: The Lemoine Circles
===================================================

.. rubric:: Chapter X: Recent Geometry of the Triangle • Articles §§ 588–596 (Folios 0276–0279, pp. 257–260)

.. picture:: hero.svg
   :alt: Triangle ABC with symmedians meeting at Lemoine point K, parallels through K cutting the sides in six concyclic points on the First Lemoine Circle, and Brocard circle on diameter OK
   :caption: Symmedians, Lemoine Point K = X(6), First Lemoine Six-Point Circle, and Brocard Circle

Constructs the two remarkable six-point circles discovered by :person:`Émile Lemoine <lemoine>` in 1873 (**§§ 588–596**, :cite:`Lemoine 1873 <lemoine:1873>`): the first and second (cosine) :term:`Lemoine circles <lemoine-circles>`.

Principal Theorems, Constructions & Identities
----------------------------------------------

* **§§ 588–592 — The First Lemoine Circle**: Straight lines drawn through the :term:`Lemoine point <lemoine-point>` :math:`K` parallel to the three sides :math:`BC, CA, AB` intersect the sidelines in **six concyclic points** whose circle is centered at the midpoint :math:`X(182)` of segment :math:`OK`!
* **§§ 593–596 — The Second Lemoine (Cosine) Circle**: Antiparallels to the three sides drawn through :math:`K` are bisected at :math:`K` and all have equal length, so their six endpoints lie on a circle centered at :math:`K` itself whose chord intercepts are proportional to :math:`\cos A, \cos B, \cos C`.

Constructive & Symbolic Connections
-----------------------------------

Both the First Lemoine Circle and Brocard Circle appear in exact symbolic precision in the ``lemoine_symmedian`` hero diagram; both belong to the Tucker circle family (§ 684).
